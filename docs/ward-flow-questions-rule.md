# The `questions` rule

**Owner instruction, 2026-08-30, in his words:**

> *"can you create a rule for all chats and this project in general that when I say questions what
> you do is list everything you need my opinion or answer on and you explain it clearly and
> succinctly and easy to skim and understanding as well as your clear recommendation"*

**This is the single home for the rule.** It is in project memory so new chats inherit it; it is not
duplicated into any spec.

## The trigger

**"questions" is a command, not conversation.** When the owner says it, stop and produce a
**complete** list of everything waiting on his opinion or decision.

## The format, per item, and nothing else

1. **A bold one-line question** — the actual decision, answerable in a sentence.
2. **Two or three lines of context** in plain English. No file paths, no function names, no internal
   detail unless it changes his answer.
3. **"My recommendation:"** — one option with its reason. **Never a menu.** If the choice is
   genuinely his — clinical judgement, product direction, risk appetite — say so, **and still say
   which way you would go.**

**Order by what costs most to get wrong**, not by what you happen to be working on.

## The rules that make it work

- **Completeness is the point, and incompleteness is the failure mode.** A question left off because
  it felt minor is the failure. **He cannot answer what he is not shown.** Include the ones you have
  been quietly working around.
- **Include questions whose answer you ASSUMED.** If you built on a guess, **the guess is a
  question.** Say what you assumed and that it is reversible.
- **Never bundle two decisions into one question.** He answers in a line; a compound question gets
  half an answer.
- **If nothing is open, say "nothing" in one line.** Do not manufacture questions to look thorough.
- **Say what is NOT a question**, when that is short enough to be useful.

## Why it earns a rule beyond his asking for it

**He is deciding across five parallel chats, and a question buried in a status update does not reach
him.** The board session's account of what prompted it: it was carrying four open questions and had
mentioned three of them once each, hours apart, inside longer messages. **That is indistinguishable
from not having asked.**

**And this session under-delivered on it within minutes of the rule being written.** Asked for "all
the questions and clarifications", it produced four — and the honest list was eleven. **The seven
missing ones were all decisions it had made on its own recommendation and recorded as reversible**,
which is exactly the category the rule names: a decision you took because nobody stopped you is a
question you have not asked.

## The check, before sending

**Walk the decisions you have recorded, not the ones you remember.** A question you resolved by
recommending it to yourself does not feel open, which is precisely why it will not come to mind. The
list is built by reading the record, not by recall.

## Confirmation by exception — when a question has already been asked

**The build session’s invention, 2026-08-30, and it solves a problem this rule creates.**

The `questions` rule says ask him. **The duplicate-question problem says do not ask him twice.** When
a second chat holds a direct instruction that a relay appears to reverse, both pull at once: it must
not build on second-hand word, and it must not make him answer the same thing twice.

**The resolution: state the rule as you understand it, say you are proceeding on it, and ask him only
to correct you if it is wrong.**

> *"Confirmation by exception, not a second question. That costs him nothing if we have it right, and
> catches it if the relay lost something in the middle."*

### The axis is whether an answer EXISTS — not how sensitive the subject is

**My first draft of this boundary was wrong, and the case that produced the technique disproves it.**
I wrote: never use it where being wrong would touch privacy. **But the five-fact rule IS a privacy
rule** — so that boundary forbids the instrument in exactly the situation it was invented for.

**The correct axis, from the build session that caught it: does an answer already exist?** He had
answered. His words were quoted, through a relay. **The only risk being managed was transmission
loss**, and restating a specific understanding for correction is the right instrument for that and
for nothing else.

### The two clauses

1. **Use it ONLY to restate a decision he has already made**, in terms specific enough that he would
   recognise his own answer as misdescribed.
2. **Never to obtain a decision he has not made** — and on a standing refusal, patient safety,
   privacy, or anything expensive to undo, **never at all, regardless of how confident anyone is
   about what he would say.**

### The test is specificity, not topic

**Silence is not an answer, and the failure mode one step away is "nobody objected, therefore
approved"** — the same shape as everything else here: an absent signal read as a passing one.

**What makes a confirmation-by-exception safe is that it states a specific thing he can recognise as
wrong. It stops being safe the moment it is vague enough that silence could mean he did not read
it.** That is the test. **Not the subject matter.**

**And it never licenses proceeding on a relay generally.** It is a way of not charging him twice for
a question already asked — not a way of turning an unanswered question into an authorisation.
