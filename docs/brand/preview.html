How it is built

glyph units, S height = 100

One stroke, then a copy of itself turned 180° about the centre pin. The outer edge of each stroke rides a single circle of radius 30. The inner edge is a throat, an elbow and a long tail sweep; each meets the next on a shared tangent, so no junction shows a kink at any size. Both tips are rounded by a small arc tangent to the curves either side of it.

What changed from the original

The small white-on-navy mark on the brand sheet is the design. Measuring it showed it was already circle-based — the outer sweep sits on a radius-30 circle to within a quarter of a unit across its whole length. This version reconstructs those circles rather than tracing the pixels, and corrects three things.

01

The two strokes are now identical. In the original the lower stroke was heavier than the upper one, and irregularly so. Making each an exact 180° turn of the other is what makes the mark a system rather than a drawing.

02

The glyph is centred in the tile. The original sat hard against the right edge — a 26% margin on the left against 9% on the right.

03

Every junction is tangent-continuous. Consecutive arcs share a tangent where they meet, so enlarging the mark reveals no kinks.
