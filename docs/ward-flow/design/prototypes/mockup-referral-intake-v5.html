Synthetic prototype

This form is not a medical device. It records a small set of structured facts about the person referred, the request itself, the written history you type below, and — when the form was opened from a person’s record — a pointer to that record: an id.

The written history is free text. It is stored and sent exactly as you type it, so anything you put in it — a name, an address, another person’s details — becomes part of the referral. Nothing checks it. The structured questions above it still cannot hold a name, and no field on this form holds a figure from the Mental Health Act.

What sending does

Sending records the request and puts it in front of a coordinator.

It places nobody: a coordinator decides every placement, one at a time.

Maya’s name is not written into the structured referral — the receiving team opens her record through the pointer, where access is theirs to have or not. Whatever you typed in the history is sent as written, including a name if you put one there.

7 · Rules the build must not break

Each of these is here because breaking it has already caused a defect somewhere on this system. Rules 6 and 8 to 11 are new in v5 and all of them exist because of the written history.

  1. Tap targets stay at 48 px. Not 44. Reducing them to satisfy a generic accessibility rule reintroduces a known test flake.
  2. The reason sits beside the Send button, never above it. Reasons appear and disappear as you answer; a reason above the button moves the button out from under a thumb.
  3. A figure beside a checkbox stays beside that checkbox, and a destination’s question stays on that destination’s card. The screen-reader announcement points at it; moving it into a rail silently unhooks the description.
  4. Absence is written in words. “Not recorded”, “Not written yet”, never a blank, never a dash, never a zero. A blank reads as a value.
  5. Nothing on this screen ranks, scores or suggests a destination, and nothing scores the history. Ordering is the network’s own fixed order, and the copy says so.
  6. The name never enters the STRUCTURED referral — and the history is not structured. This replaces v4’s rule 6, which said flatly that the name may be shown but never stored. That was true of a form with no text box and is not true of this one. Say which half is enforced, every time it is said.
  7. Every list of options is generated from the one source of truth, never retyped into a sentence. Retyped enumerations are how this system produced stale copy three times — including the sentence on this very screen that counted the permitted facts and was still saying “five” after a sixth was added.
  8. The rail reports the history; it never previews it. A truncated preview implies something read it. Report the section count and the length.
  9. Free text is never given a green state. Green means checked on this screen, and nothing checks this.
  10. Nothing truncates the history silently. Over the limit is a blocking, visible, counted state. A form that quietly drops the last paragraph of a risk note is worse than one that refuses to send.
  11. When more than one destination is chosen, the screen says the history goes to all of them, before the send, not after.

8 · Open questions the build must settle

Written down here rather than promised on screen. A screen that guarantees behaviour nobody has built is the defect this project finds most often, so none of these appears as a sentence to a clinician until it is true.

What happens to a half-written history if the page reloads? Today, on this prototype, it is lost — a reload wipes everything. That is tolerable for three checkboxes and not tolerable for four paragraphs of a risk note. Either the draft is held and the screen says so, or the screen warns before leaving. It must not silently do neither.
Can the history be edited after sending, and does the receiving team see that it changed? Unanswered. The structured referral is a decided act; free text invites correction. If it can be edited, the version the receiving team read must remain recoverable, or two people will be acting on different accounts of the same patient.
Who can read the history after it is sent, and for how long? The structured fields carry no identifying content, so access to them has been a low-stakes question. The history changes that: it is the first thing on this form that would matter if the wrong person read it. This needs an answer before the screen is used with a real patient, not before it is built.
Are the three sections the right three? Chosen as the smallest set that shapes the writing without becoming a clinical form: why now, background, risk and safety. A single unbounded box was rejected because nothing in it suggests a shape, and everything ends up in it. This is a clinical judgement and belongs to the owner.
Are the character limits right? 1500, 2000 and 1000 are placeholders chosen to be generous enough that nobody meets them in normal use, and small enough that the field is bounded. Nobody has measured a real referral against them.

Mockup only — nothing is wired, and every person, bed count, wait, median and line of history above is invented. The screen is drawn mid-flight on purpose, so answered, outstanding, not-applicable, in-catchment, out-of-catchment, refused, written and unwritten states are all visible at once. The anchor’s fields are the ones a person record genuinely holds; where a record does not hold one, the anchor says so in words rather than leaving a gap. Dark mode is defined for every token across the full triple, print and forced-colours rules are defined, and no state on this screen is carried by colour alone.
