Design decisions

  • One field, filters as removable chips, not three controls. Today's screen shows a text box, a stage select and a department select at all times, even before anything is typed. Here the resting state (State 1) is a single search box with a single “+ Add a stage or department” control; once a filter is chosen it becomes a labelled chip with its own remove button (States 2 and 3), so an applied search reads as a sentence — “Okonye, in Inner City” — rather than three boxes whose combination has to be reconstructed by eye.
  • One row shape for all three lists. Today's people, referrals and movements are three differently shaped lists (a form-fed list, run-on sentences, and a table). Here all three reuse the identical row — an id, a path, a wait figure and a worded chip — so a reader learns the shape once. Only the id prefix (PT / RF / MV) and the chip's meaning change between them.
  • A row opens the thing it names, and the person is a second, separate link. A person row's own destination already is the person, so it stays one whole-row link. A referral or movement row's own destination is the referral or movement it represents (clicking RF-206 or MV-118 opens that record) — but the person's name inside the row is a second, independently clickable, underlined link straight to the patient screen. That is what actually closes today's named defect (no route to the person at all): every result now has a route to the person, without any row lying about what it opens. The two links are real sibling anchors, never one nested inside the other, which is invalid markup; the row-wide link is an invisible layer placed under the name, so the name stays clickable in its own right and the rest of the row still opens its own record.
  • Too many matches hides referrals and movements rather than piling three long lists on top of each other. This also sidesteps the two-links-per-row pattern at exactly the volume where it would feel busiest: 42 people are already shown 8-at-a-time with one link each, and adding their referral and movement matches underneath — each carrying two links — would make the narrowing controls the least visible thing on the screen instead of the most. The panel says plainly that they are hidden and why.
