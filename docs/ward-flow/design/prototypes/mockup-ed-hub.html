Who this screen is built for

  • ED staff are the primary reader. The question this screen answers first is "my patient needs a psychiatric bed — where is that up to, and who do I chase?", so the main list is sorted longest-wait-first and every row states in words what has been tried and what is still pending. A coordinator asking "who is stuck here, and which is worst" is served by the same list and by the stuck banner and "Needs a decision" panel, without a second view to switch between — a single emergency department has too few patients waiting to justify two layouts.
  • The stuck state is the loudest thing on the page. "Every ward asked has declined and nothing is pending" is stated as a red banner in full sentences at the top of the screen, named again in the totals strip, and repeated in words on the affected rows themselves (never left to a coloured edge alone) — because this is the state where nobody is currently doing anything for the patient, and it must not read as merely another chip among many.
  • The ward ledger carries other referral destinations, so it is marked, on the panel itself, as not shown to ward staff — only to the coordinator and this department's own staff.

  • The decline reasons shown (no suitable bed, secure bed unavailable, belongs to another service, out of catchment, sex mix) are drawn from the model's real controlled vocabulary, but which referral got which reason is invented.

What is real

  • Royal Perth Hospital Emergency Department and its site code RPH, and every ward named on this page (RPH Adult Secure, RPH Older Adult, SCGH Adult Open, SCGH Older Adult, FSH Adult Secure, RGH Adult Secure, SJGM Adult Open) — all from the repository's ward-sites table.
  • The emergency department record itself holds exactly three fields — an id, a site code, and a name. No capacity, no census, no waiting list. Everything else on this screen is derived from referrals whose destination names this department, and from movements recorded as originating here.
  • The decline-reason vocabulary (no suitable bed, sex mix, secure bed unavailable, belongs to another service, out of catchment, and their siblings) is the model's real fixed list — a decline can only ever give one of these reasons, never free text.
  • The privacy rule that a ward cannot see a patient's other referral destinations, while a coordinator can, is a real rule in the model; this screen is built for the coordinator-and-ED-staff audience accordingly, and says so on the panel that would otherwise leak it.
