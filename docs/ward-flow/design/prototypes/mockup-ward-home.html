Every figure here is invented

  • Every "Referrals waiting" and "Escalated" count, on every card and in the totals strip — 22 referrals waiting and 5 escalated network‑wide, and the per‑ward splits (3/1 RPH Adult Secure, 2/0 RPH Older Adult, 4/1 SCGH Adult Open, 1/0 SCGH Older Adult, 2/0 FSH Adult Secure, 5/2 FSH Older Adult, 1/0 ARM Adult Open, 2/1 SJGM Adult Open, 2/0 RGH Adult Secure). Nothing in the repository yet links a referral to a destination ward, so these are placeholders for a count the real screen would need to compute the same way as the bed figures.
  • "Updated 2 minutes ago" and the "Live" marker. Nothing here refreshes.

What is real

  • All nine ward names, their hospital, cohort and security, and the North Metro / East Metro / South Metro health‑service grouping and order — from this repository's ward-sites.ts, the same source the app's own ward index reads.
  • Every bed figure — each ward's total beds and its free‑bed count (1 of 20 for RPH Adult Secure, 1 of 14 for RPH Older Adult, 2 of 24 for SCGH Adult Open, 1 of 16 for SCGH Older Adult, 3 of 18 for FSH Adult Secure, 0 of 12 for FSH Older Adult, 2 of 19 for ARM Adult Open, 1 of 16 for SJGM Adult Open, 1 of 14 for RGH Adult Secure) and the network totals (12 of 153 free, 1 of 9 wards full) — computed by the exact formula the app's own unitCapacity() uses on every ward's own screen: the free count is the smaller of that unit's confirmed‑empty and confirmed‑allocatable numbers, both real fields on the unit record. This page performs that same calculation once and shows the result; it does not hold a second, independently‑typed figure that could drift from the ward screen.
  • Each card's link goes to that ward's real route and unit id in this prototype.

Cards, not rows — and other decisions this mockup made

  • Cards. Nine wards is a small, fixed set, and the job here is choosing one ward to enter, not scanning a column to compare forty of them. A card lets the free‑bed figure sit large and alone, and gives every ward its own unmistakable "Open ward" action — a table row would have compared better but reads as a report, not a door.
  • "Full" is a word, never only a colour. FSH Older Adult reads "0 free of 12 beds" and the word "Full" beside it, in the same red as the card's edge — a reader with no colour perception loses nothing. Wards with one or two free beds say "Low — act soon" in amber; three or more say "Space available" in green. Those three wordings, not the colours, are what a screen reader or a printed page still carries.
  • The brief named eight wards; a ninth is real. RGH Adult Secure (Rockingham General Hospital, South Metro, 1 free of 14 beds) is in ward-sites.ts and belongs on this page. The brief's "6 hospitals" was correct all along — it was the ward list that was short by one, not the hospital count that was wrong. Checked against the seed file rather than assumed from the two numbers disagreeing.
  • The totals strip is additive, not requested verbatim. The brief asked for per‑ward counts; the four‑tile summary at the top (mirroring the existing "Board" language's totals component) is my addition so a reader gets the "at a glance" answer the owner asked for before scanning any card. It carries the same two safeguards as the per‑ward figures: at most two amber‑flagged tiles, and the same single calculation reused, not re‑derived.
