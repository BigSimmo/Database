What this page cannot tell you

  • Whether this list is complete A person appears here only if their admission points at a referral this page can find. An admission whose referral cannot be found is missing from every list here, exactly as if there were no referral at all, and is counted in the 84 above rather than shown.
  • Whether somebody is still with this team Nothing records a team closing a person — no team discharge, no episode end, no closing date. So “admitted while already with this team” means this team had accepted them and nothing records that ending, which is not the same statement and must not be read as one.
  • Whether anyone is being followed up The field exists and some admissions carry a value, but no screen reads it and nothing here can set one.
  • Which referrals this team raised A referral records that its source was a community service, but the source side carries no team name — so nothing says which team raised one. Referrals addressed to this team are different: they carry the name, which is how everyone above is on this page. No list is shown until it is the real one.
  • Everyone in this area A person appears here only because a referral named this team. Anyone whose referral asked only for a bed or an emergency department, and anyone admitted with no referral at all, is on no team’s page anywhere. Expect that to be most of the ward — naming a community team is something a referrer does rarely and deliberately. Where a person lives is not read at all.
  • Any date Every date in this prototype is invented, so the page states how long ago or how long until, never the day itself.

What is real, taken from the repository

  • The team name, and that it is one of four spellings of one service. “Inner City Clinic” is a follow-up clinic named by the S2015 catchment table, reproduced exactly. On 2026-09-05 the owner ruled that it, Inner City, ICC and Inner City (central) are the same team — 21 suburbs. That is a person’s decision on record, not a similarity the software worked out, and the two are deliberately kept apart so a reader can tell them apart.
  • Every fact this page refuses to state. The follow-up field being unreadable, referrals-raised-by being unattributable, association coming from the team written on the referral rather than from where somebody lives, and dates being withheld — all four are the real behaviour of the current model, quoted from the page they already appear on.
  • The bed states. “In the bed” and “a bed is pulled — not yet arrived” are the model’s own two occupied states.
  • That “admitted while already with this team” can be derived at all. A destination records that this team accepted and when it decided, and an admission records when the bed began, so “accepted before the bed began” and the gap between the two are both real. That a team closing somebody is NOT recorded is equally real — checked in the model, not assumed — which is why the heading says what this team accepted rather than who is still with it.
  • That a decline is a recorded answer and not a lock-out, and that a coordinator can override any answer on this page.

What is invented

  • Every referral — RF-104, RF-112, RF-118, RF-121 — with its waiting time, urgency, age band, source and transport need.
  • Every admission — AD-2218, AD-2231, AD-2240, AD-2255, AD-2266, AD-2274, AD-2291, AD-2302, AD-2310 — with its unit, bed state, length of stay and expected-back wording.
  • All six figures in the header strip, and the count in “left the ward another way”.
  • Which three of the six were admitted while already with this team, the “3 of 6” split, and the three gaps between acceptance and admission — 118, 62 and 23 days, with the totals 159, 88 and 35 that follow from them. The derivation is real; these particular people and numbers are not.
  • The 84 unmatched admissions. The method behind it is real; the number is not.
  • ⚠️ An earlier draft said nine suburbs name this clinic. One does — Noranda. A review computed it; the catchment table has exactly one matching row. The invented figure was properly declared as invented and still made the page weaker, because it hid the very thing the page is arguing: a clinic named by a single suburb is visibly a one-off entry in a document. The real figure now stands in the panel above, and it is not invented.
  • Every elapsed time on the page: 3h, 19h, 1d 6h, 2d 4h, 2 days, 3 days, 6 days, 11 days, and the day counts 4, 12, 18, 26 and 41.
  • “Live · refreshed a moment ago” in the bar. Nothing here is live.

What this edition changes, and why

  • The team’s own queue is now first and above the fold. The old page never asked who was waiting on this team — the one question a community team opens a page to answer — and opened instead with people already discharged.
  • The caveats are gathered, not scattered. Four advisory paragraphs used to sit between the heading and the first fact. The ones that qualify a specific list now sit under that list, and the rest are in one rail panel a reader can consult rather than wade through. An earlier draft of this line claimed every sentence survived, and a review found three that had not — the incomplete-list caveat, the ward’s-own-plan sentence, and the other-departures sentence, which had been replaced by a version that stated where two people went when the record does not say. All three are restored above. A claim to have lost nothing is exactly the claim nobody re-checks, which is why it is written here as a caught defect rather than quietly repaired.
  • A new section the old page had no equivalent of: who was admitted while this team already had them. It is the team’s own relapse signal, it is derivable from the acceptance time and the bed’s start, and it sits above the general admitted list because it is the smaller and louder group. It is worded as what this team accepted, never as who is still with it, because no field records a team closing anybody.
  • A team switcher, because there are 65 names, and it separates the two things a reader must not confuse: entries that read alike by spelling and are not merged, and entries a person has ruled are one service. ⚠️ This copy said the opposite until the ruling landed — it cited ICC as the example of a name the check misses, which stopped being true the moment somebody decided it. Nothing went red. The pin on ICC passes either way, by design, because the alias table is deliberately unreachable from the spelling rule. A peer told me; no gate could.
  • Nothing was added that the record cannot support. No caseload, no staffing, no contact details, no map, no outcome — each of those would have been invented rather than read.
