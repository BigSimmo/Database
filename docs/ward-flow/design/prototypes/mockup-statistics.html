Correction: three quantities were being called one thing

The wait chart previously carried a single sentence naming a page-wide longest and shortest "wait for a bed", ranking all three groups against each other. It was wrong, and not only in wording: the 62-hour figure it called the longest wait for a bed belongs to Bunbury community team, and a community team has no beds. The page’s own chart label already called that same column "waiting for first contact". The 12-hour figure it called the shortest wait for a bed is time a person was held in an emergency department.

Why this was a safety correction rather than a phrasing one. This system already holds the rule that a referral must show what it is for, because a row with no stated purpose is indistinguishable from a bed request — recorded in ward-referrals.ts as "a safety rule rather than a presentational preference". Collapsing three purposes under one name is that same conflation one level up, in a chart: a reader comparing 62 hours against 12 hours believes they are seeing the same thing measured at two places, and they are not. Nothing this system measures has a shortest bed-wait of 12 hours.

The three quantities are now named separately, summarised separately, and never ranked against one another: time from accepting a referral to the bed being ready (wards), time from referral to first face-to-face contact (community teams, no bed involved), and time held in the department awaiting an inpatient bed (emergency departments). The page-wide "longest" and "shortest" markers are gone, including the two inside hover text, where a superlative is easy to leave behind.

The headline figures strip carried the same defect and is corrected too. One tile read "Median wait, referral to a bed — 27h" — a single number averaging all three quantities. ⚠️ No label could make that true, so it was not relabelled: it is now "Median wait for a bed, wards — 26h", recomputed from the nine ward figures on this page alone. A second tile called a community team’s figure the "longest wait" while the tile beside it defined a wait as "referral to a bed"; it now says "longest wait for first contact".

⚠️ One limit on the 26h, stated rather than glossed: it is the median of nine per-ward medians, not the median across patients. Those are different statistics and can differ. The tile says "middle of the nine wards" for that reason, and the caption no longer claims a seven-day window the figure cannot support.

Every figure here is invented

  • Every number on every chart: all median wait times (58h, 44h, 33h, 30h, 26h, 22h, 20h, 19h, 15h by ward; 62h, 40h, 38h, 34h, 28h, 24h, 21h, 18h by community team; 26h, 24h, 21h, 18h, 15h, 12h by ED), the 26h ward-only median in the figures strip, and the 3d 2h "longest wait for first contact right now". The 26h is not an independent invention: it is the median of the nine invented ward figures above (15, 19, 20, 22, 26, 30, 33, 44, 58), so replacing those replaces it.
  • Every decline count: the 102 total, all six reason counts (34, 21, 18, 14, 9, 6) and every percentage share, and every ward/team/ED decline count in both breakdown tables and the league table.
  • Every discharge-blocker count: the 86 total, all six blocker counts (27, 19, 16, 11, 8, 5), and every ward/team/ED breakdown of it, including the "held for review" ED counts.
  • "Wards with zero declines this month: none." All nine wards are invented as having declined at least once.
  • "Updated 2 minutes ago" and the "Live" marker, and the RF-140 referral reference. Nothing here refreshes.
  • Every "most common reason" and every reason label attached to a ward in the league table.

What is real

  • 9 wards across 6 hospitals — RPH Adult Secure, RPH Older Adult, SCGH Adult Open, SCGH Older Adult, FSH Adult Secure, FSH Older Adult, ARM Adult Open, SJGM Adult Open, Rockingham General — and 6 of the 8 named emergency departments in this repository’s ward model. ⚠ The screen omits Joondalup Health Campus ED and Peel Health Campus ED. Neither has an inpatient ward, so both vanish from a list built by walking hospitals — which is how this panel was built. A panel headed “by emergency department” that shows six of eight without saying so reads as complete and is not. The rebuilt screen must cover all eight or state the exclusion on the screen itself.
  • 16 community team names and every suburb count shown — Midland 68, Bunbury 48, Joondalup 36, Bentley 32, Peel 27, Rockingham 23, Osborne 19 — counted from the catchment table built from five WA Health documents covering 537 suburbs.
  • The hospital a ward or ED belongs to.

Decisions this mockup made

  • The charts are built in CSS, not SVG — bars, a faint repeating grid line and a labelled axis, all using the copied colour tokens. This avoids the SVG dark-mode risk (a hard-coded stroke disappearing) entirely, since every colour resolves through the same custom properties the rest of the page uses.
  • "Which wards decline the most" is the only ranking kept off the front page, per the owner's ruling. The ward breakdown table earlier on the page carries wait and blocked-discharge counts only, deliberately not a decline count, so it cannot double as the same league table under a different heading.
  • The emergency-department column on the discharge-blockers chart is read as "held for a medical review in the ED before going home" — a real but minor blocker category, distinct from the community and accommodation blockers that dominate the total. This is a judgement call the brief did not spell out; flag it if a different reading is wanted.
