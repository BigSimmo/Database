Why this page exists next to the ward home page

  • The home page answers two questions: what can this ward offer, and what needs an answer from it. It is read in seconds, several times a day, and it is where a ward confirms its numbers.
  • This page answers a third: who is in every bed, and what is happening to them today. It is where a ward works — confirming discharges, recording where someone is going, noticing a date that has moved three times, and finding the person who has been here 156 days.
  • Nothing is duplicated between them by accident. The bed map appears on both because both questions need it, but here it carries a stay, a band, a sex, a home region and every marker, and it is filterable and sortable. On the home page it carries only state.

⚠️ One thing this page proposes that the model cannot do today

  • 🔴 This page said a bed cannot be joined to a patient. That was wrong, and the correction matters. Admission carries no patient field, which is true — but it carries referralId, and Referral carries an optional patientId. The chain Admission → Referral → Patient exists and resolves today.
  • It is optional on purpose, and that is the real design constraint. The reducer’s own comment on the line that sets it reads: “undefined here means the referral was raised without a person on file — which is a real case, not a gap to be filled. Inventing an id to avoid an empty field is how a referral comes to point at the wrong human being.” So some occupied beds have no linked person, permanently and correctly, and a board that walks admissions expecting names will draw blanks that read as missing patients.
  • Which is why this panel must name its two absences separately — a bed filled without a referral is a different fact from a referral raised before anyone was on file, and one blank for both would hide the difference. Reported by Ward Builder One, confirmed here independently against the reducer.
  • Two fields are deliberately absent. The patient record also holds Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander status and interpreter language, and the code marks both as not settled for display, pending the Aboriginal health review. Interpreter need is the one worth settling for this screen, because a ward board is exactly where it would be acted on. Neither is drawn here.
