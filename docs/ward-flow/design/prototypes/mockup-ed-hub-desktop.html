The 24-hour figure

This department's own access target — a performance measure it is judged on. It is not a Mental Health Act deadline, and nothing on this page is a legal clock.

The two clocks

In department runs from triage, the earliest moment the record holds. Since referral runs from when psychiatry was asked. Somebody may have been here a while before either started.

Who this is for

The department's own team. No bed is ever allocated automatically; a human here confirms every step, and nothing on this screen ranks one person above another.

What is real

Every figure and identifier above was read off the running prototype at /mockups/ward-flow/ed/rph-ed on 2026-09-04 — RF-009, WF-003, WF-304, WF-320 and WF-312, their waits, tiers, destinations and states. The eight department names and the ward names come from ward-sites.ts. The 24-hour departmental target is the screen's own.

What is invented

The layout only. Two figures are computed here and shown nowhere in the current screen: nearest target breach (10h 36m, the screen's own remaining-time figure surfaced as a total) and the here-no-bed-requested grouping. The three sentences in the attention panel are written for this mockup; the facts inside them are the screen's.

Design decisions

Direction is carried by an arrow in every row, so inbound and outbound never depend on the heading. Every explanatory paragraph the current screen sets above its content has moved into a .blurb under the panel title or into the band at the foot, so the reader meets the data first. Empty panels state what the absence means. At most two totals are flagged.
