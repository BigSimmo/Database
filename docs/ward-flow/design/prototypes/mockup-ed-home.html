Every figure here is invented

  • All eight departments' figures: everyone-waiting counts, longest-wait durations, detained counts, "declined by every ward" counts, and the two departments named as past the 24-hour target — 34, 1d 6h, 14 of 34, 7, and 2 of 8 in the totals strip, and every count in the hero panel and the three health-service groups.
  • Every movement id (WF-231, WF-244, WF-249) and every patient name. Patient names are invented and deliberately unlike any real list.
  • "Updated 2 minutes ago" and the "Live" marker. Nothing here refreshes.
  • The choice of Royal Perth Hospital as worst. A real deployment would compute this from live referrals and movements; here it was chosen so the ranking rule below has something to demonstrate.

What is real

  • All eight hospitals, their emergency department names and site codes, and their health-service grouping — from this repository's ward-sites.ts: Royal Perth Hospital, Sir Charles Gairdner Hospital and Fiona Stanley Hospital (each with its own named ED), Armadale Hospital ED (on the Armadale Health Service site), St John of God Midland ED, Rockingham General Hospital ED, Joondalup Health Campus ED and Peel Health Campus ED. East Metro, North Metro and South Metro are the model's own HealthService values.
  • The Mental Health Act legal-status wording — "Referred for psychiatric examination", "Detained awaiting examination" and "Involuntary inpatient" — is the model's own LegalStatus type, not invented phrasing.
  • The 24-hour figure is a real, named constant (ED_ACCESS_TARGET_MINUTES, 1440 minutes), counted up from when a movement opened. The model is explicit that this is a departmental access measure, not a Mental Health Act deadline, and this page follows that: "past 24 hours" is worded as an access-target breach, never as a legal one.
  • "Declined by every ward" uses the model's real closed set of decline reasons (DECLINE_REASONS) and the real shape of a decline record (unit, time, reason) — only the count on this screen is invented.
  • That the department record itself holds only an id, a site code and a name is exactly what EmergencyDepartment in the ward model contains — nothing else. Every figure above is derived from referrals and movements that point at a department, never read off the department itself.

Design decisions

  • Ranking rule. Departments are ordered first by whether anyone there has passed the 24-hour access target, then by how many patients have been declined by every ward asked, then by the raw number waiting. Royal Perth Hospital leads on all three at once, which is what makes it the unambiguous worst rather than a close call between two departments.
  • Only one card shouts. The brief warned that eight amber cards is worse than none, so only Royal Perth gets the hero treatment, and it uses the crit (red) token rather than the signal (amber) token the two flagged totals use — one tier more severe, so it never competes with them for attention. The other seven departments carry their state in words and a chip, never in an escalated background.
  • At most two amber-flagged totals. "Longest single wait" and "Declined by every ward" are the two flagged tiles in the totals strip; every other figure, including the hero panel's own figures, is unflagged by design.
  • Grouping by health service, not by severity. The remaining seven departments are grouped East Metro / North Metro / South Metro, matching how the ward screens group units, rather than re-sorted worst-first a second time — the ranking argument was already made once, by the hero card.
  • Fiona Stanley is the honest second-worst. It is also past 24 hours, so the "Needs a decision" panel names it explicitly in words rather than letting one red card imply every other department is fine.
