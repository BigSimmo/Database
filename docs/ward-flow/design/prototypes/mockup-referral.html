What changed, and why

  • Twelve equally-weighted cards became four numbered steps. Numbering is honest here because this genuinely is a sequence: step 4 cannot be answered until step 3 is, since the legal answer decides which wards may take the person.
  • Four questions disappeared entirely. Age band, sex, suburb and catchment come from the record when the form is opened from a person. A fact read from the record cannot be mistyped into it.
  • Ineligible wards stay listed with their reason and an "Ask anyway". Hiding them would be the easier design and the wrong one: the coordinator can override eligibility, and a screen that removes the option silently removes the authority. The reason is stated in words, never by colour alone.
  • The right-hand panel surfaces facts and makes no judgements. That is a deliberate limit, agreed before this was designed: it may tell you a referral is already open, and it may not tell you where to send this one. The moment it suggests a destination it is making a clinical decision, and the moment it fills a field an unreviewed guess becomes a record.
  • Every option she qualifies for is now in the right-hand panel, with a search. Step 4 shows only what has been CHOSEN. Discovery sits beside the record because that is where the facts that decide it are — her legal status, her age, the open referral — and commitment sits in the form. One control, not two: the panel is where you tick, and step 4 is what you have ticked.
  • The panel groups options by whether they fit, and states why in words. “Meets all four requirements”, “also possible — a department rather than a bed”, and “cannot take her, and why”. The clinician sets the order and the panel ranks nothing: eligibility is a rule the model computes, choosing is a clinical decision, and the panel does the first and refuses the second.
  • ⚠️ An ED card claimed “a notification, not a bed request — nobody declines it”. That was false, and it is corrected here. Checked against src/components/ward-management/ward-referrals.ts: the spec’s FD-18 correction of 2026-08-30 says “every referral is declinable, the ward’s medical notification included”, and that stating a referral’s PURPOSE is “a safety rule rather than a presentational preference”, because a declinable row with no stated purpose is indistinguishable from a bed request. The card would have told a clinician a referral cannot come back declined when it can. Both ED rows now state the purpose and say nothing about who may decline.
  • “Inner City team” left the “also possible” group. It sat under a heading a reader skims as available while its own text said it was not, so a clinician could tick it and be refused — the confusion the refusal exists to prevent. It has its own heading rather than joining “cannot take her”, because nothing is wrong with the team: it is her own, and discharge planning is simply answered at step 4.
  • The count read “6 of 9” above ten destinations. Both figures were wrong. Counted: three meet all four requirements, two departments can take the referral, one is arranged at step 4, four cannot take her. Ten listed, five available now.
  • ⚠️ The order was distance, under a caption saying the panel ranks nothing. Choosing distance IS a judgement about what matters most, and first position reads as the answer whatever the caption says — it put RPH Adult Secure (1 free, 11 km) above FSH Adult Secure (3 free, 22 km), and for a detained patient needing escorted transport that trade-off is the clinician’s to make rather than inherit. The order is now hers to set. The default is alphabetical, because every option in that group already meets all four requirements, so ordering by any clinical quantity puts a thumb on a scale the panel says it is not touching; a name carries no such signal.
