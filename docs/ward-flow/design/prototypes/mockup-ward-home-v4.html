What this edition changes, and why

  • 🔴 A sentence that was false is gone. The previous edition said a bed becomes ready only once released, “which is why that figure is always one you can fill this minute”. One of the three was bed 17, pulled for WF-355 — and the bed board, locked the same night, calls that same bed taken, not free. Both pages were internally consistent and one of them told a clinician something untrue. The figure is unchanged; the promise around it is corrected, the tile carries the pull, and the fact leads the attention list.
  • The tile label is now “Ready”, not “Ready to fill this minute”. Not a preference: CAPACITY_FIGURE_LABELS.availableNow is already the word Ready, so the mockup now says what the software will say.
  • One alarm colour on the figures strip instead of four. The design language’s own rule is that amber means “look here” and loses all meaning if everything is amber; the previous edition toned four of five tiles. Out of service is a standing fact, not an alarm, and keeps its red where the bed is drawn.
  • The heading says what the ward is in a sentence. Six equal chips made the hospital’s name read as loudly as the locked/open split, which is the one identity fact that decides a placement. The split stays a chip; everything else became prose.
  • The heading’s status became a route. “1 of 3 answered today” was a chip that told you something was outstanding and gave you nowhere to go. It now says the same thing and takes you there.
  • The referral decisions moved out of the 19–21rem rail into the middle column. They are the most consequential act on the page — accept, decline, and a seven-option reason — and the rail is the narrowest thing on it. The rail now carries only what is read rather than acted on.
  • Six bed tiles to a row once the three-column layout is in, and one tile height for all eighteen. The bay is 12 and the open group is 6, so six across makes each group a whole number of rows; automatic fitting was giving five, which split 12 into 5, 5 and 2. Two tiles also carried a note repeating their own group heading (“can hold a detained patient”), which made them taller than their neighbours — the notes are gone and the bottom edges line up.
  • “Today on this ward” is full width, under the figures. A day reads across, not down, and in the rail its events would have had to be truncated. It carries the six figures that travel together and the day as recorded — and deliberately no roster, ward round or meeting, because this ward records none of those and an invented schedule would have been the most believable thing on the page.
  • “Where to refer” sits under the discharges, not in the rail. The owner’s one-way rule says the community referral is raised when discharge is being planned, so it belongs where somebody is already standing when they need it. All four answer states are drawn, including the two that refuse to give one answer.
  • 🔴 A print defect is patched here that belongs in the shared block. The canonical block re-points its colours for printing on :root and :root[data-theme="dark"], and its own comment explains that the second selector is needed because a rule on :root alone loses on specificity. The same argument defeats it once more: the dark palette is written to :root:not([data-theme="light"]), which is equally specific and earlier in the file, so a reader in dark mode who never touched the theme control — the default — prints the entire dark palette, including near-white text onto white paper once the printer drops the backgrounds. Measured in a browser with the outcomes written down before the run. A second half: the block re-points thirteen neutral tokens and none of the twelve state colours, so a green note box prints near-black for every dark-mode reader.
    ✅ Both are now fixed in the canonical block, at the owner’s instruction on 2026-09-05, and the copy that was in this file has been deleted rather than left behind. The rule is one checkable sentence — for print, every token takes its light-theme value — and it reaches all four second-edition files, because the block was edited at its source and the three mockups were re-cut from it. Verified by hash: the canonical prefix is identical in design-language.html, both v3 mockups and this one. The hoisted fix was itself proved against a control that reproduced the old selector list and showed the old, wrong answer.
    ⚠️ The ten first-edition mockups in this folder have no print block at all, so a dark-mode reader printing any of them still gets the dark palette. That is a different thing from the defect above — nothing there was half-fixed, there was never a fix — and it is recorded rather than repaired, because it is ten files outside what was asked for.
  • The route to the bed board is more prominent, at the owner’s request. Both places it appears are now the same primary button with more room around the words, a heavier weight and an arrow — the panel header’s version was a quiet outline before. And the two were saying different things: the bar read “Open the full bed board” and the panel header read “Open the full board” for one destination, which is how a reader comes to believe there are two. One name now.
  • Three smaller repairs. .asked was added by the previous edition with a wash background and a color-mix border, and under forced colours it loses both — it now has a drawn edge, along with everything this screen adds. The <ul class="asks"> wrapper was styled by nothing in any block, so the class is gone rather than left looking load-bearing. And “Coming in” now names the bed WF-355 was pulled into, so the same fact is not stated in two places without a way to tie them together.
  • Unchanged from the locked edition, on purpose: the bed map is still the page, the accent is still PsychSift’s own primary, surfaces are still crisp white, the switcher still groups 23 real wards by hospital, and DESIGN-LANGUAGE.md still governs Ward Flow and is untouched.
