Why this is empty

Nothing is missing from the record. Nothing has been drawn. Royal Perth’s wait list and chart were built out in full as the worked example. Drawing the other seven the same way would mean inventing seven more day-by-day trends and seven more named individuals, and a prototype that invents that much detail is one somebody will eventually read as a measurement.

This department’s headline figures are not missing — waiting now, median wait, longest wait, how many have passed 24 hours, and how many were accepted by a ward in the last 7 days are all in the comparison table below, alongside every other department.

What is invented

  • Every count, wait time, distribution, ward acceptance and the 30-day trend on this page: the 68 people waiting and their five-band distribution; the 30-day median-wait line for all departments and for Royal Perth Hospital; every reference in the two waiting-now tables and the ward each has or has not been accepted into; every “where they went” count; and every figure in the department comparison table, including its totals row.
  • The “as at 11:42” liveness marker at the top of the page.

A department is not a ward

This screen only ever describes people currently standing in a department: how many, how long, and where they went next. It never shows a bed count, an occupancy figure or a length of stay for the department itself — those belong to the ward screens, and an emergency department has none of its own.

What a 0 means, and what a dash means

A 0 in a count or a band is a genuine measured answer — nobody in that band, nobody placed out of area. A dash, or the words “not drawn in this prototype,” mean no figure was produced at all. The two must never be read as the same thing.

A zero anywhere on this page is a real, measured zero unless a caption beside it says the figure could not be taken. It is never used to mean “not tracked.”
