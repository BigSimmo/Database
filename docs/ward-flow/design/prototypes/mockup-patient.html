Every figure and every name here is invented

  • The patient: "Marguerite Sowden", MRN 048213, date of birth 14 Mar 1987, age 39 — invented and deliberately unlike any real list.
  • The address: "No. 42" is an invented house number. No street name has been invented for it — only the suburb, Ashfield, is real (see below).
  • Every clinical fact: the three admission dates, wards stays and working diagnoses; the three medications and doses; the three past medical history entries and their onset years; the legal status, its review date, "open to the team" status and last-contact interval; the GP name and clinic name; the interpreter/language and Aboriginal/Torres Strait Islander status values.
  • All four referral history entries (RF-041, RF-029, RF-017, RF-009), their outcomes and their dates.
  • "Record created" and "last updated" dates, and both items in "Needs a decision".
  • "Jordan Smith" in the new-person form is placeholder example text, not a record.

What is real

  • The suburb Ashfield and its catchment team, Midland — a verified pairing from this repository's catchment table (`ward-catchment.ts`).
  • The wards named — RPH Adult Secure, SCGH Adult Open, FSH Adult Secure — and the team name Midland Community Team, from `ward-sites.ts` / `ward-catchment.ts`.
  • The four fields in the masthead (name, record number, date of birth, age) are the only fields the model holds today; every other field on this screen is newly approved and not yet built.
  • Mental Health Act 2014 (WA) is the real name of the governing legislation; the specific order and review date attached to it here are invented.

How this screen is organised

  • The masthead deliberately shows only today's four fields. Everything the owner has newly approved sits in the "Identity and key facts" panel directly beneath it, so the screen itself marks the line between what exists now and what was added.
  • Past admissions carry no workflow chip. The urgent/routine/stalled/accepted vocabulary belongs to open work; a closed admission from 2021 is not "routine" in that sense, so it is described in the row's own text instead of borrowed colour.
  • The referral history panel is headed, worded, and positioned as coordinator-only, and referral information appears nowhere else on the page — wards see only referrals into or out of themselves, never this cross-service history.
  • A second state models the more common case: no previous admission. Below the main record, "No previous admission" shows the same "Past psychiatric history" panel worded as a positive fact ("No previous admission to a WA mental health ward") rather than left blank. An empty panel reads as a failed load, not as a first presentation, and a first presentation is a large share of psychiatric admissions — not an edge case.
  • Interpreter/language and Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander status sit inside the same plain facts list as address and GP, with no icon, colour or card of their own — unchanged from before. Their order in that list changed. They previously sat adjacent to each other, with Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander status last in the list, directly above the Past psychiatric history panel. Side by side, and immediately preceding a history panel, that placement formed a cultural-identity cluster that invited a reading no clinical record should invite — that the status was context offered for the history that followed. The fix is ordering only: the fields keep their exact wording and styling, interpreter/preferred language now sits with GP among the other contact and communication facts, and Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander status sits earlier in the demographic group, no longer last and no longer beside the language field. A future edit that "tidies" this back into alphabetical or original order would reopen the adjacency this fixes.
  • Every count on this page was checked for singular/plural agreement (the panel counts, the admission and referral wait figures, the contact intervals). None currently reads a number against the wrong grammatical form — if this screen is ever wired to live data and any of these becomes exactly one, its noun must switch to the singular ("1 admission", not "1 admissions"), matching the convention already used elsewhere in this set.
