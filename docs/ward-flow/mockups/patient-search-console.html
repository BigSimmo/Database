This search is not a medical device. What this page will not do

Design decision

No risk, acuity or triage score, and no clinical priority sort. The sentence at the top of this page promises the search never assesses risk, acuity or treatment. A sort on clinical need would break that promise, so the only orderings offered are operational facts: elapsed time, recency, name, number.

No "best match" and no "top result". A superlative asserts that a comparison happened. The order key is named above the list instead, so a reader can see what decided it.

A referral is never rounded up to a destination. Destination shows a ward that has accepted. Where none has, the row says so in words rather than borrowing the first ward that was asked.

Closed and arrived records never appear — and the stage filter says that out loud rather than quietly omitting the option.

What is real, taken from the repository

  • All eight people and their record numbers. Talia Halloway UM100001 and Marcus Hallowin UM100002 are the real seeded pair, and their near-identical family names exist deliberately so that related-spelling matching has something to catch. Searching hallow really does return both.
  • The seven movement stages and their exact labels — placement requested, destination review, accepted awaiting bed, bed pulled, handover ready, moving, arrived — from stageCopy.
  • That arrived and closed movements never appear. The search filters to open records first and unconditionally, so a closed movement cannot reach a row and be described as still open.
  • "Open for", not "since arrival". The elapsed figure is measured from when the movement was opened, and the record holds no arrival instant at all, so calling it arrival would understate every wait shown.
  • That destination reads only an accepted ward. A movement with referrals out and no acceptance shows no destination, deliberately — the running screen already works this way.
  • The three record kinds and their order — people, then referrals, then movements — and the reason: a referral is somebody still waiting for a decision.
  • The governance sentence at the top is the one the running screen carries today, word for word, including "queued referrals", which was added to it on 4 September.
  • The add-a-person route, and that what was typed carries across into it rather than being retyped.

What is invented

  • Every movement and referral on the page — WF-411, WF-388, RF-2291 — with every department, destination, owner, elapsed time and decline count attached to them.
  • Every figure in the yield strip, every facet count, and the four recent searches.
  • The whole timeline in the preview pane, including all four timestamps.
  • The age bands, home regions and the "put to 3 wards" count. The fields exist; these values do not.
  • That Talia Halloway has an open movement at all. The person is real in the fixture; the movement tied to her here is drawn for this mockup.

Two tokens this edition adds

  • A matched-text highlight (--mark, --mark-ink). A search page cannot work without one and the system had none. It is the accent quietened, not yellow: yellow is the only hue here that would not already mean something, and a highlight in a meaningless hue starts reading as a warning.
  • A selected-row surface (--picked, --picked-edge), because a preview pane needs the row it is showing to be identifiable at a glance.

What this edition proposes, and why

  • Search is the page, not a field on it. The old screen opened with a heading and three small dropdowns. This opens with the box, at the width of the page, with the result count already in it.
  • Facets with live counts replace blind dropdowns. A dropdown asks you to guess what is in there; a facet list shows you, including the zeroes, which is how you learn that nothing came from Bunbury today.
  • The figures describe the search, not the ward. Every other board in this product opens with the ward's state. A search result set has its own shape and that is what a person needs after typing.
  • Those figures are one object, not five cards. The first draft gave each a border, a radius, a shadow and a 3px coloured cap — five separate things, and five colours competing at the top of the page before a single result had been read. They are now one strip divided by hairlines, and colour is spent once, on the longest-open figure, because that is the only number here that means somebody has waited too long. The rest state their condition in a word and mark it with a 6px dot.
  • Each figure is a filter. A number you can see and cannot act on makes you retype what it already knows. Clicking "still waiting" narrows to those records.
  • One way of writing a duration. The unit is set smaller and quieter than the number it qualifies, in the strip and in every result row, so 2d 6h reads as one quantity rather than four glyphs of equal weight. The first draft rendered it in full-size tabular figures and the space between the two halves opened up far enough to read as two separate numbers.
  • One person, more than one record. A person, their waiting referral and their open movement are the same human being, and the old page listed them in three places without ever saying so. The tie is drawn in both directions.
  • A preview pane. Checking six results meant six round trips. Now the record opens beside the list and the list keeps its place.
  • The idle state teaches. "What you can search" is on the page permanently rather than hidden in a placeholder nobody reads, because the searchable fields are not guessable.
  • The refusals are on the screen, not only in the code. What a clinical tool declines to do is part of what it is, and burying it in a comment means only the people who wrote it ever know.

Open questions for the owner

  • Should recent searches persist at all? They are a record of who was looked for. Useful, and also a trail. Drawn here as browser-local and one-click clearable, which is a proposal rather than a decision.
  • Is "Nothing attached" the right words for a person known to the system with no referral, movement or bed? It is accurate and it may read as a judgement about the person rather than about the record.
  • Should the tie between a person and their movement be shown at all on a search screen, or is that a fact for the person's own page?
