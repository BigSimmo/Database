What is invented

  • Which bed holds which person, every length of stay from 3 to 156 days, and every home region except bed 09's.
  • Every time and date on this board: 08:15 and 07:40 on bed 09, 04:50 on bed 07, 09:01 on bed 17, 27 July for bed 09's arrival, 12 August for bed 14's lapsed date, and the whole of Since yesterday.
  • The three moves of bed 09's discharge date, and the statement that it is the most moved date on the ward.
  • The person in bed 09, whose name follows the standard's own rule for invented names: an uncommon given name and a word for a plant. No record number, UMRN, address, phone number or clinician's name is drawn anywhere on this page.
  • Which two beds are the ward's two held beds. The ward's row in the network's data records two held beds and no reason for either. Bed 07 is drawn as one of them because its own occupant is away at an emergency department. The second is bed 10, and the board says that no reason is recorded rather than filling one in.

What is real

  • The ward: FSH Adult Secure at Fiona Stanley Hospital, 18 beds, 12 locked and 6 open, male only, an authorised hospital, and its ready, held, confirmed, expected and blocked counts, all read from the same ward row the Command screen reads.
  • The people coming in: both are movements from the network's own data, with their own tier, wait, legal status and emergency department. Nothing about them is written here that Command does not hold.
  • The hospital sites, the health services and the community team names are real Western Australian names from the repository's tables. Joondalup Health Campus and Peel Health Campus have an emergency department and no inpatient ward, so neither appears in the switcher.
  • The seven leaving destinations, the admission states, the home regions, the discharge date moved count, the blocker list, the away at an emergency department state, and the rule that a pulled bed is taken rather than free.

What was changed to agree with Command

  • The day and the clock. The source board stood at 21:51 on a different day. This page stands where Command stands: Saturday 15 August 2026, 10:42 AWST, day shift, handover at 14:00. Every duration on the source board is kept and its clock time moved back to fit, so bed 09 is 2h 27m past its time rather than 7h, and bed 17 was pulled 1h 41m ago as before.
  • The ward's own partition. The source drew 14 occupied beds and no held bed. The ward row holds 12 occupied and 2 held, so beds 07 and 10 are drawn as held and the board's eighteen tiles now sum to the row. The occupancy line reads 12 men and no women, which is the row's own sex mix.
  • Bed 09's discharge is confirmed, not merely expected. The ward row records one confirmed discharge today and none expected. The control that read Confirm the discharge is therefore They have left, because the confirming has already happened and the bed has still not been released.
  • Specialling. The source said both of this ward's two specialling shifts were in use. The ward row records two free, and the board follows the row.
  • The people coming in are the network's own. WF-355 and WF-361 were invented for the source board. The two movements accepted to this ward in the network's data are WF-031, whose bed is pulled and who is moving, and WF-002, accepted with no bed pulled. Both are drawn here under their own identifiers.
  • Red is a breach and nothing else. The source drew an overdue discharge and a lapsed date in red. Both are amber here, which is the standard's look here, and red is kept for the blocked bed.
  • The switcher counts what is drawn. It said 23 wards and 15 hospitals and listed four. It now lists every ward the network's data carries, with its own ready count, and says how many of the network's twenty-three wards are drawn.

Why this page exists next to the ward home page

  • The home page answers two questions: what can this ward offer, and what needs an answer from it. It is read in seconds, several times a day, and it is where a ward confirms its numbers.
  • This page answers a third: who is in every bed, and what is happening to them today. It is where a ward works, confirming discharges, recording where someone is going, noticing a date that has moved three times, and finding the person who has been here 156 days.
  • Nothing is duplicated between them by accident. The bed map appears on both because both questions need it, but here it carries a stay, a home region and every marker, and it is filterable and sortable. On the home page it carries only state.

One thing this page proposes that the model cannot do today

  • An earlier draft said a bed cannot be joined to a person. That was wrong, and the correction matters. Admission carries no patient field, which is true, but it carries referralId, and Referral carries an optional patientId. The chain from admission to referral to patient exists and resolves today.
  • It is optional on purpose, and that is the real design constraint. The reducer's own comment on the line that sets it reads: "undefined here means the referral was raised without a person on file, which is a real case, not a gap to be filled." So some occupied beds have no linked person, permanently and correctly.
  • Two fields are deliberately absent. The patient record also holds Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander status and interpreter language, and the code marks both as not settled for display, pending the Aboriginal health review. Neither is drawn here.
