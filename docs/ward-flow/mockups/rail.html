    The rail, open and closed

    Built to the design system, second edition

    Open, 236 pixels

    • The brand, and a hairline in the service hue beneath it when one service is chosen, so the rail says where you are.
    • The shift: a ring filling towards handover, the time left in mono, and a meter.
    • Every screen in three groups. Where a screen has a state, one line of text says it under the name: breached and due soon under Command, free and none under Capacity, the oldest under Referrals, the time left under Handover.
    • The current screen's own sections beneath it, each with a count, each a jump.
    • Pinned: the movements the coordinator is watching, with the tone bar of the row and the wait in mono.
    • Who is signed in, whether the figures reconcile, and the note on what is invented.

    Closed, 76 pixels

    • A glyph and one word per screen, the counts as small tags, red only where a legal deadline has passed and amber where the oldest referral is over two hours.
    • Rest the pointer on any glyph and a card names the screen, says what it is for and gives its state line, so nothing is lost by closing.
    • The shift as a ring around the coordinator's initials, and the pinned movements as a fly-out with a count.
    • The reconcile dot stays. The note moves to the footer of the page.

    What was added, and why

    • Text under the screens. A count says how many, a line says what kind. Together the rail reads as an instrument, not a list.
    • Tone on the tags. A red count is a breach and nothing else. Amber is look here. Nothing else in the rail is coloured, so those two are seen.
    • The service stripe. One service chosen is easy to forget and expensive to forget, so the rail carries it.
    • The bracket key flips open and closed from anywhere, and the choice is remembered. The width animates in a fifth of a second, and not at all when motion is reduced.
    • The hover card in the closed strip, so the strip can be used all shift without opening it.

    Considered, not built

    • Two lines per screen with its purpose. Good for a first week, noise by the second. The hover card and the tooltip carry the purpose instead.
    • Bed pressure meters by service in the rail. They belong in the Activity drawer's live tally and on Capacity, where there is room to read them.
    • The on call roster. Roles only would be safe, but it changes by site and by hour, and a wrong roster in a rail is worse than none. It belongs in Tools with a time it was read.
    • A search box in the rail. The bar already has one, and one composer per page is the rule.

    Every figure here is invented: the 23 movements, their identifiers, tiers, waits, deadlines, owners and destinations, the four referrals, the bed counts, the four overrides, the pinned movements, the shift and the on call roster, the notices, the activity and the clock.

    Every person's name is invented, built from uncommon given names and words for plants, birds and stones so that none matches a real person. The contact tables show placeholder extensions and addresses. No real phone number, address or record number appears anywhere on this screen.

    What is real: the eight emergency departments, the hospital sites, the health services and the ward names, from the repository's own tables. Six of the seventeen sites are drawn here.
