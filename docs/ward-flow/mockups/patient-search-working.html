Patient search

One box for a person, a referral waiting for a decision, and a movement already under way. Records that have left the system are not here, and the page says so rather than leaving you to wonder.

Reads live · 8 people, 3 referrals, 6 movements

This search is not a medical device. It looks up people already known to this synthetic system by name or record number, queued referrals, and open movements by id, department, destination, stage and owner — it never assesses a patient's risk, acuity or treatment, and a movement that has already left the system (closed or arrived) never appears here.

    What this page will not do

    Design decision

    No risk, acuity or triage score, and no clinical priority sort. The sentence at the top promises the search never assesses risk, acuity or treatment. A sort on clinical need would break it, so every ordering offered is an operational fact: elapsed time, recency, name, number.

    No "best match" and no "top result". A superlative asserts a comparison happened. The order key is named above the list instead, so you can see what decided it.

    A referral is never rounded up to a destination. Destination shows a ward that has accepted. Where none has, the row says so in words rather than borrowing the first ward that was asked.

    Closed and arrived records never appear — and the stage filter says so out loud rather than quietly omitting the option.

    What is real, taken from the repository

    • All eight people, with their real record numbers and dates of birth. Talia Halloway UM100001 and Marcus Hallowin UM100002 are a deliberately seeded near-spelling pair, as are Marrowby/Marrowbee and O'Quinn/Oquinn — they exist so related-spelling matching has something to catch.
    • The matching rule itself. A record number matches on any part of it; a name matches from the start of the given or family name; punctuation and case are folded away first. That is the real behaviour, not an approximation of it.
    • The seven movement stages and their exact labels. Arrived is one of them and is deliberately absent from the filter, because an arrived movement has left the system.
    • That destination reads only an accepted ward. A movement with referrals out and no acceptance shows no destination.
    • "Open for", not "since arrival" — the record holds no arrival instant, so it cannot say one.
    • The three record kinds and their order — people, then referrals, then movements, because a referral is somebody still waiting for a decision.
    • The governance sentence at the top, word for word as the running screen carries it today.

    What is invented

    • Every movement and referral — six and three of them — with every department, destination, owner, timestamp and decline count attached.
    • The four emergency departments and four wards as a working list for this page.
    • Every timeline entry in the preview pane.
    • The age bands, home regions and decline counts. The fields are real; these values are not.

    Three things this build adds beyond the mockup

    • A movement can now be found by its patient's name. The running screen searches movements by id, department, destination, stage and owner only — so searching a name found the person but not the journey they are on. This is a real addition and it needs a decision, not just a build.
    • The clock is pinned to 5 September 2026, 09:00. Every elapsed figure is measured from that instant, so this page reads the same tomorrow. An unpinned demo clock is a defect this project has already shipped once.
    • Facet counts are computed against the other filters, not against everything. Tick a department and the stage counts drop to what that department actually contains — so a count of 0 means "nothing here", not "nothing anywhere".

    Still open for you

    • Should recent searches persist at all? They are a record of who was looked for.
    • Is "Nothing attached" right for a person with no referral and no movement? It is accurate, and it may read as a judgement about the person rather than the record.
    • Should the person-to-movement tie appear here, or only on the person's own page?
