    What is in the header

    Built to the design system, second edition

    Title row

    • The page title, the prototype mark and the fixed disclaimer.
    • Search across movements, departments, wards and owners. Press the slash key to reach it. It refuses risk and acuity scores, a best match, and closed or arrived movements, and says so.
    • The scope switcher: all sites, or one department, grouped by health service with the waiting count beside each.
    • New referral, the one primary action, with the source chosen first.
    • Who is signed in and the shift, then More: print the handover sheet, open the design system, set the appearance, sign out.

    Statistics strip

    • Six figures that matter now, derived from the open movements on every render.
    • Red on a breach above zero and nothing else. Amber means look here, on at most two figures.
    • The clock with the date and AWST.
    • All figures opens the full set in a dropdown so the strip never crowds: the network now, by department, by health service, by tier, beds by site, and the reconciliation line.
    • When the window is narrow the strip scrolls sideways under a soft shade instead of wrapping.

    Outstanding tasks bar

    • Every piece of open work, worst first: deadlines passed, deadlines within two hours, movements with no owner, declines to answer, acceptances with no bed pulled, referrals awaiting triage, overrides for governance review, the handover sheet.
    • Each task is a filter. Press it and the queue shows only those movements, and the filter is stated in words above the list.
    • A task with a count of zero is not outstanding and is not shown. When nothing is outstanding the bar says so.
    • Escape closes a menu first, then clears the task, then the scope, then the search. Every change is announced to a screen reader.

    Every figure here is invented: the 23 movements, their identifiers, tiers, waits, deadlines, owners and destinations, the four referrals, the bed counts, the four overrides, the clock and the shift.

    What is real: the eight emergency departments, the hospital sites, the health services and the ward names, from the repository's own tables. Six of the seventeen sites are drawn here. No phone number, address, record number or person's name appears anywhere on this screen.
