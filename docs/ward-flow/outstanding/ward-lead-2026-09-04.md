# Outstanding after the 2026-09-04 fold

Written at the point where three builder branches and `origin/main` were folded into
`codex/task-ward-flow-live-state-20260831` and the full ward suite went green. Everything below is
**known and deliberately not done**, not forgotten. Each entry says who found it and what would
settle it.

None of these blocks a push. Several of them block calling the prototype finished.

---

## 1. Nine misleading-copy rows are still unfixed

Two of the eleven verified rows were repaired on 2026-09-04 (the capped `eligibleCount` on
`flow-diagram.tsx`, and the Destination columns on the handover and patient-search screens). **Nine
remain.** They were deliberately not handed out during the fold, because a list handed over is a
list somebody has to re-derive, and the two that were done both changed shape on contact with the
code.

⚠️ **The rule established while doing the first two, and it is the important part of this entry:**

> A sweep's output is a population to READ, never a population to CHANGE.

Of four `destinationUnit` call sites assigned as "apply the same fix", one wanted the opposite fix
and two were already correct. **A sweep that replaced every call would have broken three working
screens.** Whoever takes the remaining nine should open each one before changing it and expect the
count to shrink.

## 2. Seven of nine control predicates omit a precondition their reducer enforces

**One load-bearing guard, four screens, and the layer meant to back it up is wrong on seven of
nine.** Worth fixing. Not an emergency, and an earlier version of this entry said it was.

🔴 **RETRACTED, BECAUSE THE CORRECTION IS THE MORE USEFUL HALF.** This was first written as "an
enabled button on a closed movement that silently does nothing". Measured: at the tip it was
reported against, the officer's job list already filtered `movement.closure === undefined`, so a
closed movement had no row and no button. The window was real — the filter landed at `3ea069b6c`,
about an hour before the finding was written — and it had closed. **The reachability claim came from
an integrator's brief, was taken on trust because it arrived with certainty, and was never
re-measured by anyone.**

**What is measured and stands:** of nine movement-scoped predicates across four files, **seven omit
a precondition their corresponding reducer case enforces.** The officer's four
(`TRANSPORT_ACCEPTED`, `TRANSPORT_EN_ROUTE`, `PATIENT_COLLECTED`, `PATIENT_ARRIVED`) were repaired
on 2026-09-04. `examinationBlockedReason` and `bookTransportBlockedReason` (ED) and
`pullBlockedReason` (ward) remain. `handoverBlockedReason` and `withdrawReferralBlockedReason` do
check closure.

Three further functions — `declineReasonBlockedReason`, `referralDraftBlockedReason`,
`transportAnswersBlockedReason` — are **draft validators with no reducer counterpart** and should
not be counted alongside the mirrors.

⚠️ **What actually prevents a phantom control today is a list filter written elsewhere in the same
file** — `ed-screen.tsx:799` filters `!movement.closure`, `ward-screen.tsx` filters through
`isOpen`. **Delete a filter, reuse a screen, or add a control outside the list, and the phantom
returns with no defence from the predicate layer.**

The guard shape exists and is committed (`tests/ward-officer-blocked-reason-parity.test.ts`): for
each predicate, drive every movement it permits through the corresponding reducer case and assert
none is rejected. Driven over the fixture, never compared by reading — **a reading-based check is
another hand-written mirror and inherits the same defect.** Floor the permitted population.

⚠️ **Why they were not found:** `arrivedBlockedReason` carried a comment claiming it "mirrors
`case PATIENT_ARRIVED` exactly, including the floor guard". It does mirror the floor guard. The
specific thing it named as evidence of completeness was true, and it omitted closure. **A mirror is
a copy that nothing checks**, and a comment enumerating what it covers reads as an inventory.

## 3. `hasRecordedReferral`'s first disjunct is dead code

In `coordinator/shortlist-panel.tsx`:

    hasRecordedReferral = recordedDestination !== undefined
                       || Boolean(acceptedUnitId)
                       || referredUnitIds.length > 0

**The first disjunct can never be the one that makes it true** — anything that resolves
`recordedDestination` already satisfies a later disjunct. Redundant, not wrong.

Deliberately left, and deliberately **not** given a code comment saying "this clause is dead": that
comment decays the moment the clause stops being dead, and nothing local would fail. Recorded here
instead. Found by Ward Builder Three.

## 4. `KNOWN_BACKLOG` conflates two different things, and one row proves it

`tests/ward-primitives-shared.test.ts` keeps `search/search.module.css: .field`. That row is
correct: `search/.field` now `composes:` the shared rule and keeps only its own `color`, but
composition does not remove a declaration, so the scan still sees the name twice and is right to.

⚠️ **"Duplicate" is therefore doing two jobs in that list** — a second independent copy of a rule,
and an extension of the shared one by composition. **They differ in what somebody must DO: the
first is a merge, the second is nothing at all.** The list cannot currently tell them apart.
Flagged by Ward Builder One.

## 5. `wards/ward-overview.module.css` renders nowhere — retention is the owner's call

It is the single remaining row in `KNOWN_UNFIXED` in the print-coverage guard, exempt for a
structural reason rather than unfinished: **no importer anywhere in the repository.** Fixing its
print coverage would be work on a file nothing mounts; deleting it is not an integrator's decision.
**This needs a yes or no from the owner, and it is the only thing in this document that does.**

## 6. `ward-management.module.css` gets no composition blanket, deliberately

The `composes` resolver grants a file-wide coverage blanket only for a ROOT edge — the first
class-declaring rule, and the only edge composing that (class, target) pair. `ward-management.module.css`
composes `wardTokens` twice, so it gets none, and its print coverage rests on its own block rather
than on composition. Its DOM containment was never verified the way the seven composing screens
were. **A conservative choice, endorsed, and recorded because a future reader will otherwise find
it surprising.**

## 7. Seven orphaned exports in `morning-page.tsx`, and only two of them matter

`NoHandoverYet`, `ViewControl`, `ViewButton`, `viewButtonActive`, `viewActiveMark`, `viewExplainer`
and `noHandoverButton` are unwired remnants of a removed fixed/live toggle and a no-handover branch.
Itemised in `docs/ward-flow/unwired-symbols-2026-09-01.md`.

⚠️ **The actionable half is the asymmetry, not the count.** `.viewControl` is `display: none
!important` inside `@media print`, so four of them are protected by a real mechanism rather than by
being dead. `noHandoverTitle` and `noHandoverButton` sit outside it. **So re-wiring the toggle puts
zero unreset declarations into a printable DOM, and re-wiring `NoHandoverYet` puts two.**

**A row that is safe because the code is dead is not the same as a row that is safe.**
