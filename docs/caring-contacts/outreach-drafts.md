## 1. Lived-experience review of the message set

Closes hazard **H-04**. Facilitation material is in [message-review-pack.md](message-review-pack.md).

**Who to approach.** Three or four people with lived experience of a suicidal crisis or of hospital
discharge after one — through a consumer advisory group, a lived-experience workforce lead, or an
established peer network rather than personal clinical contacts. Nobody currently under your care. Offer
proper payment or recognition at the usual rate for consumer participation.

**Suggested subject:** Feedback wanted on text messages for a suicide-aftercare project

> Hello [name],
>
> I am a psychiatrist working on a project that would send short, supportive text messages to people over
> the twelve months after they leave hospital following a suicidal crisis. The messages are one-way — they
> carry no questions, no advice and no monitoring, and they sit alongside normal care rather than replacing
> it.
>
> I am not asking you to test software. I am asking for something more important and much earlier: whether
> the words are right. The messages have never been read by anyone with lived experience, and I would
> rather find out now that they read as automated, or cold, or intrusive, than after a service has approved
> them.
>
> It would be about [ninety minutes], either as a group or individually, whichever you prefer. I would show
> you the messages as they would actually arrive on a phone and ask what you make of them — how they land,
> whether the boundary about replies feels like care or like distance, and how the final message after a
> year should be different. [Payment/recognition arrangements.]
>
> There is one specific question I would particularly value your view on. The messages come from a number
> nobody reads. If someone texts back during a bad night, they get an immediate automatic reply saying so
> and pointing to a staffed phone line and crisis support. I want to know whether that is experienced as
> honest or as a door closing.
>
> No decisions have been made and nothing has been built for real patients. Your feedback would shape the
> content before it goes anywhere near approval.
>
> Would you be willing? Happy to answer any questions first.
>
> [Name, role, contact]

**Before the session:** confirm the messages you will show are the current ones, and read §1 of the review
pack — the reply wording was corrected on 19 August and the replacement is provisional, awaiting exactly
this input.

## 2. Hospital referral feed feasibility

Closes hazard **H-44**, the largest programme risk. Question set is in
[referral-feasibility.md](referral-feasibility.md).

**Who to approach**, in order of usefulness: a digital health or clinical informatics lead in the candidate
health service; the mental health service manager who might sponsor a pilot; anyone who has recently taken
an external clinical system live in the same service — they know the real path rather than the documented
one.

**Suggested subject:** Quick feasibility question — structured discharge referral to an external follow-up service

> Hello [name],
>
> I am scoping an aftercare project and I need a reality check from someone who knows our systems before I
> take it any further.
>
> The idea is a caring-contacts service: brief one-way supportive text messages sent by a hospital aftercare
> team over the twelve months after a person is discharged following a suicidal crisis. There is a recent
> Australian randomised trial supporting the approach, and I have a working prototype with the clinical and
> privacy model already written down.
>
> Everything depends on one thing I cannot answer myself. Could a discharge in [system name] send a
> structured referral to an external service — patient identity, discharge date and time, mobile number,
> and a flag that the mobile is the patient's own — and receive a structured outcome back?
>
> Three specific questions:
>
> 1. Which system would originate that referral, and does it support structured outbound referrals to an
>    external service at all?
> 2. Is there a field recording that a mobile number is the patient's own and suitable for discreet
>    contact? A plain mobile number field is not enough for this purpose, and I would rather design around
>    reality than assume.
> 3. Who owns the decision, and what approvals would be needed before any external system received patient
>    identifiers?
>
> I am not asking anyone to commit to anything. If the honest answer is that a structured feed is not
> realistic in a sensible timeframe, that is genuinely useful — the fallback is manual entry by the
> aftercare team, and I would rather design for that deliberately than discover it late.
>
> Would twenty minutes be possible in the next few weeks?
>
> [Name, role, contact]

**After the conversation:** record the answers in `referral-feasibility.md`, update hazard **H-44**, and if
manual entry becomes the likely route, write it up as a dated decision-lock revision rather than letting it
drift into the design.

## What not to send yet

Do not circulate the [evidence brief](evidence-brief.md) or the
[demonstration script](demo-script.md) more widely until the message set has been through the
lived-experience review. The strongest version of this proposal is one where the first question a clinical
director asks — "has anyone with lived experience seen this?" — already has an answer.
