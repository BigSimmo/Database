My recommendation

A — tabs with the list control at the end.

Why the tabs at all, over the chip that is there now
The chip answers "where am I" and hides "where else could I be". On a page that runs to four screens, the second question is the one worth answering on screen. Tabs answer both, in the site's existing treatment, for six more pixels.
Why the control at the end
Because the row scrolls. Contacts nearly fits; Logistics does not, and a section you cannot see is a section that does not exist. The control is bordered, carries the number of sections, and turns its chevron over when open — three signals that it is a button before anybody taps it.
Why not B's "On this page" label
It costs about 90 px of a 390 px row — two visible tabs instead of four, and the counts off the tabs to make room. The row's job is legible from the underline and from the fact that the words in it are the headings you can see below. If it turns out not to be, B is a small change from A.
What it changes in the code
The section rail this repository already ships (InPageSectionRail, the document viewer's own) is this row; On Call would mount it at phone width instead of the single chip, and the chip's sheet becomes the control's sheet. The weighted progress track is then redundant — the tabs are a better map — so it goes.
