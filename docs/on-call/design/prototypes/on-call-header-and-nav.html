On Call — one header, and one place for each kind of navigation

Three things on the Contacts screen do a job something else on the same screen already does. The chip row names the page's groups; so does the header's jump list. The header paints the page's name; so does the bar above it. The two bars of chrome are drawn as two, with a seam, when they are one thing. These boards remove all three.

The division of labour. The pill moves you between On Call's pages, and opens with a way back to every other mode. The page row beneath it moves you around the page you are on. Nothing else navigates. Every board is Contacts at 390 px unless it says otherwise.

  • The weighted track hangs off the bottom edge of that one block, which is what makes it read as the header's own progress rather than as a third bar.

What these boards change

Four removals and one addition. Nothing gains a new control.

1 · The filter chip row goes, on all three pages that carry one
Contacts, Referrals and Orientation. The header's jump list already names the same groups, and it moves the reader to one instead of hiding the others. This is a deliberate departure from boards 06, 08 and 10 of the original drawings, which all show a chip row.
2 · The group moves onto the title line
As a chip rather than a second line, so the page row is 48 px instead of 64 px and the group reads as something you can change.
3 · The two bars become one block
One surface, one bottom rule, the weighted track hanging off its lower edge.
4 · Referrals and Orientation gain groups
Their tags become group headings, so every page in the mode works the same way and the header always has something to jump between.
5 · The switcher is unchanged
It already opens On Call's pages with a back control to all modes. Board 02 records it so the three surfaces can be judged together.
