**Status:** evidence record, 2026-08-22. Offline only. **RAG impact: no retrieval behaviour

**Source evidence:** the Gate E capture of 2026-08-21 — 60 real answers in

## 1. The headline

Ledger row `#231` reads as a timeout problem: _"Live answers degrade to source-only when
`answerRouteBudgetMs.fast` (25000) binds while retrieval is healthy."_

The capture does not support that framing on any of its three load-bearing claims:

| `#231` as written                          | What the 60 answers show                                                                |
| ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The 25 s **fast** budget is what binds     | It binds in **3 of 60 case-runs**. Half the timeouts are on the 35 s **strong** budget. |
| ~20 of 30 answers are the defect           | **12 of 30 never call the model at all.** The real population is 9–10.                  |
| Timeout and quality-failure are two faults | They are **one fault** — the quality-retry ladder — wearing two labels.                 |

And the capture surfaced a defect `#231`'s framing cannot see at all: **the two worst answers in
the whole set carry no fallback reason, no gate reason and `degraded_mode.active: false`.**

Recommendation: re-scope `#231` to the retry ladder, and split two new rows out of it. Detail in
§6.

## 2. The premise: the fast budget binds in 3 of 60 case-runs

Route budgets are fixed at route-selection time — `answerRouteBudgetMs`
(`src/lib/rag/rag-route-budget.ts:4`) is indexed by the mode `chooseAnswerRoute` returned, and
`createAnswerRouteDeadline` is called exactly once, at `src/lib/rag/rag.ts:2839`, before any
generation. So the budget a case gets is decided by its **first** routing token.

Mapping every `provider_timeout` instance in the capture to that token:

| case (run)                            | first routing token                 | mode   | budget |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------ | ------ |
| `benzodiazepine-agitation-dose` (v18) | `clinical_risk_or_complex_query`    | strong | 35 s   |
| `ect-source-gap-specific` (v18)       | `clinical_fast_grounded_synthesis`  | fast   | 25 s   |
| `lamotrigine-rash-action` (v18)       | `strong_routine_retrieval`          | fast   | 25 s   |
| `bulimia-definition-simple` (v19)     | `strong_routine_retrieval`          | fast   | 25 s   |
| `quetiapine-dose` (v19)               | `medication_dose_risk_strong_route` | strong | 35 s   |
| `mirtazapine-dose` (v19)              | `medication_dose_risk_strong_route` | strong | 35 s   |

`strong_routine_retrieval` is `mode: "fast"` despite its name — it is the final fallthrough at
`src/lib/rag/rag-routing.ts:1288`. That naming is worth knowing before reading any routing log.

**Three of six timeouts are on the 35 s strong budget.** Raising `answerRouteBudgetMs.fast` to
35 s would not reach them, and for the other three it would buy additional rejected retries
rather than answers (§4). This is the same conclusion README §1.2 item 5 and HANDOVER §1 already
record from the 35–40 s probes; the capture is independent confirmation of it from a different
direction.

## 3. Three populations hide behind one label

`answer_quality_tier: "source_only"` is a presentation label, not a failure signal. Split by
what actually happened:

| population                                                                                          | v18 | v19 | can a budget change reach it? |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --- | --- | ----------------------------- |
| **A.** Provider never called (`provider_attempted: false`, `observed_provider_request_id_count: 0`) | 12  | 12  | No                            |
| **B.** Generation attempted, then discarded — the real `#231`                                       | 9   | 10  | Partly                        |
| **C.** Correct abstention (`acamprosate-renal-limits`, `unsupported` route, 0 sources)              | 1   | 1   | No                            |
| **D.** Model answer delivered                                                                       | 8   | 7   | n/a                           |

Population A is identical across both runs, which is what you would expect from a deterministic
provider-free path.

### 3.1 The eight identical `source_only` cases split 4 / 2 / 2

This was the single biggest open question in the brief. The answer is not uniform.

**Four are correct — leave them alone.** `lithium-monitoring-documents`,
`long-acting-injectable-documents`, `patient-safety-plan-documents` and `nocc-document-support`
literally ask _"what documents / sources support X"_. Each was routed
`source_support_document_lookup` and the routing chain **ends there** — no fallback token, no
gate reason, no retry. Each returns, e.g.:

> I found 5 indexed documents that support this query: Lithium (CAMHS); Bipolar Disorder In
> Adults; ACMHS And OACMHS Triage To Discharge SOP (EMHS); …

A source list is the right answer to a source-list question. **These four are not `#231`.**

**Two answer a clinical question with a document list.** `duress-pathway` (_"What is the duress
procedure pathway?"_) and `agitation-im-route` (_"When is IM medication used in the agitation
pathway?"_) receive the same _"I found 5 indexed documents…"_ list. Both are clinical questions;
neither asked which documents exist. `duress-pathway` was classified
`query_class: document_lookup` and `agitation-im-route` was routed
`high_confidence_extractive_retrieval`. No gate fired for either. Wrong answer shape, silently.

**Two ship incoherent prose, grounded, with nothing flagged.** Verbatim from v18 and v19
(byte-identical in both):

- `antipsychotic-metabolic-monitoring` — _"What metabolic monitoring is required for
  antipsychotics?"_ →
  > The guidance for metabolic is that compliance, monitoring and evaluation.
- `discharge-documentation` — _"What discharge documentation is required?"_ →
  > The guidance is that aim > To effectively identify admission and discharge processes to
  > facilitate ease of access to > 2.

Both are `grounded: true`, `citation_count: 5`, `degraded_mode.active: false`, no
`fallback_reason`, no `generation_quality_gate_reasons`. **Every metric built on
`fallback_reason` — including `#231`'s own framing and the Gate E blinded tally — scores these
two as clean answers.** They are the worst output in the capture.

### 3.2 Structural reachability gap, but not yet a demonstrated remedy

The first-choice extractive branch runs from `src/lib/rag/rag.ts:3002` to `:3182`. It contains
exactly one answer-quality check, and it is guarded on ungroundedness at `:3107`:

```ts
const extractiveNeedsReviewFallback =
  !finalizedAnswer.grounded &&
  extractiveReviewCitations.length > 0 &&
  !(finalizedAnswer.routingReason ?? answer.routingReason ?? "").includes("comparison_evidence_gap");
if (extractiveNeedsReviewFallback) {
  const extractiveQualityReason =
    generatedAnswerQualityFailureReason(finalizedAnswer, args.query, queryClass) ?? …
```

`generatedAnswerQualityFailureReason` (`src/lib/rag/rag-extractive-answer.ts:3252`) owns
`incomplete_opening_sentence`, `bad_final_answer_quality`, `fragment_like_answer`,
`low_yield_answer`, `template_like_answer` and the rest — and it is only ever called **inside**
that block. A grep of `:3002`–`:3182` returns exactly one call site.

So: a grounded first-choice extractive answer bypasses this call site. That is a code-path fact, **not** proof that widening the call reaches a remedy for the two examples.

The focused predicate check described in PR review was run with the two quoted answers and their actual eval query classes (`medication_dose_risk` and `document_lookup`): `generatedAnswerQualityFailureReason` returned `null` for both. The current predicates therefore accept these incoherent answers. Moving the call outside the grounded guard alone would not catch them.

What the examples establish is a **predicate-strictness gap**. What remains unproven is whether another grounded extractive answer already fails an existing predicate, which would separately establish a reachability fix. Do not prescribe the guard-only change until a focused test supplies that evidence.

### 3.3 Three more cases fail without a provider

`adhd-medication-monitoring` (`final_quality_gate:source_gap`), `olanzapine-lai-monitoring`
(`claim_support_high_risk_gap`) and `form-required-documentation`
(`source_backed_review_fallback`) all have `provider_attempted: false`. They were routed
extractive by design, built an extractive answer, and _that_ answer was rejected — reaching the
review fallback through the ungrounded branch above. No generation, no timeout, no budget. A
route-budget change cannot touch them either.

## 4. The timeout label masks two mechanisms

The capture supports a quality-retry-ladder diagnosis for cases where an answer returned and was rejected. It does **not** establish that every `provider_timeout` is a retry-ladder failure.

### 4.1 Response-bearing timeouts support the retry-ladder mechanism

- **`lamotrigine-rash-action` carries the same gate reason under both labels.** `missing_query_overlap` in v18 (labelled `provider_timeout`, 2 provider responses) and in v19 (labelled `generation_quality_failed`, 3 responses).
- **`mirtazapine-dose` v19 is labelled `provider_timeout` and carries `bad_final_answer_quality` with 1 response.** An answer returned, was rejected, and the retry then ran out of clock.
- **The delivered answer is byte-identical either way** for cases that moved between buckets. The labels do not tell the reader whether an answer was attempted.
- **The timeout sets have zero overlap between runs** on identical inputs, which is consistent with a sequential 1–3-call ladder racing a fixed deadline under ordinary provider-latency variance.

For this subset, a larger budget can move cases between labels without improving the delivered fallback. It supports investigating retry admission control, not raising `answerRouteBudgetMs`.

### 4.2 Zero-response timeouts are a separate unresolved path

Three listed timeout instances had **zero provider responses**: `benzodiazepine-agitation-dose` v18, `ect-source-gap-specific` v18 and `quetiapine-dose` v19. No completed answer existed for a deterministic quality gate or retry to reject. Retry admission control therefore cannot explain or fix this subset.

The dumps contain no per-attempt timing or response telemetry. Before attributing these cases, capture the initial-attempt lifecycle: route deadline, provider request start/end, response count, quality-gate result and retry decision. Until then, `provider_timeout` is a mixed label, not one root cause.

### 4.3 The response-bearing ladder has two structural findings

**(a) There is no budget admission control on the quality retries.**
`deadlineAllowsGenerationRetry` (`rag-route-budget.ts:57`, requiring
`generationRecoveryReserveMs + minimumGenerationRetryMs` = 22 s remaining) is applied to the
_truncation_ self-heal only, at `rag.ts:3542`. The quality escalations —
`fast_quality_retry_strong` (`rag.ts:3623`, generating at `:3660`) and `strong_quality_retry`
(`rag.ts:3709`, generating at `:3721`) — call `generateWithModel` unguarded. A grep of
`rag.ts:3600`–`:3730` for `routeDeadline`, `remainingMs` or `deadlineAllowsGenerationRetry`
returns nothing.

**(b) The one time-guard on the strong repair can never fire.** At `rag.ts:3698`:

```ts
if (answerNeedsStrongQualityRepair && generationLatencyMs >= generationTotalBudgetMs) {
```

with `generationTotalBudgetMs = env.OPENAI_ANSWER_TIMEOUT_MS * 2` = **60 000 ms**
(`rag.ts:3342`). Inside a 35 s route deadline, 60 s of generation latency is unreachable.

### 4.4 What the response-bearing ladder costs

From `openai_usage`, aggregated per case. Input tokens spent on answers the pipeline then
discarded:

| run | discarded input | total input | share   |
| --- | --------------- | ----------- | ------- |
| v18 | 64,223          | 191,354     | **34%** |
| v19 | 97,465          | 202,481     | **48%** |

Token counts only. `estimated_cost_usd` is null in both dumps, so this is deliberately not stated in dollars.

## 6. Recommendation — re-scope `#231` and split two rows out

**Nothing here is shipped in this session.** Each item below is a separate packet with its own
gates.

### Packet 1 — re-scoped `#231`: admission control on the quality-retry ladder

Give the two quality escalations the same deadline admission check the truncation self-heal
already has (`rag.ts:3623`/`:3660` and `:3709`/`:3721`), and recalibrate the dead 60 s guard at
`rag.ts:3698` to the route deadline it actually runs inside. Effect: a doomed retry is skipped and
the source-backed recovery is returned immediately instead of at the deadline — the same answer,
sooner, with less discarded spend.

Explicitly **not** part of this: raising `answerRouteBudgetMs`. §2 and §4 both argue against it,
and HANDOVER §1's standing constraint forbids it without evidence that rebuts the recorded probe
result. This diagnosis reinforces that constraint rather than rebutting it.

Protected surface. Behaviour change. **Needs a live eval-canary pair.**

### Packet 2 — identify the predicate gap before changing reachability

Do **not** move `generatedAnswerQualityFailureReason` outside the grounded guard as a guard-only change. The two demonstrated incoherent answers return `null` when the current predicate runs with their actual query classes, so that change alone would not catch either one.

First, add focused tests that make the desired clinical answer-quality outcome explicit. Then diagnose the missing predicate or threshold and measure its effect on the eval suite. A separate reachability change is justified only if a grounded extractive example is shown to fail an existing predicate but bypass that call site. Protected surface. Behaviour change. **Needs a live eval-canary pair.**

### Packet 3 — clinical questions answered with a document list

`duress-pathway` and `agitation-im-route` (§3.1). Route selection / query classification, not
generation. Lower priority and needs its own diagnosis; do not fold it into Packet 1 or 2.

### Measurement change — no code

Stop reporting a single "source-only rate". It sums three unrelated populations (§3), and in this
capture it concealed the two worst answers entirely. Report `provider_attempted: false` separately
from generation fallbacks, and count §3.1's three sub-groups apart.

## 7. What this analysis could not establish

Stated plainly so the next session does not inherit these as settled:

- **How close any case came to its deadline.** The dumps carry **no latency fields at all** — no
  `route_deadline_exceeded`, `total_latency_ms` or `generation_latency_ms`. The structural finding
  in §4.1(a) makes that measurement confirmatory rather than decisive, but it is a real gap. A
  latency-instrumented re-capture (~$0.66, estimated from token counts) was offered to the owner
  on 2026-08-22 and declined as unnecessary for this diagnosis; it may be worth revisiting inside
  Packet 1.
- **Whether a grounded extractive answer already fails an existing predicate while bypassing this call site.** The two quoted incoherent answers do not: the focused predicate evaluation reported `null` for both. The next task is predicate strictness, then a separate reachability decision.
- **Reproducibility.** Two runs is not a distribution. The 34% / 48% discard figures in §4.2 are
  two samples, not a rate. A third repeat capture (~$0.66) was also offered and declined.
- **Clinical adequacy of the four correct document-list answers.** They answer the question asked;
  whether the corpus behind them is right is a different question.
