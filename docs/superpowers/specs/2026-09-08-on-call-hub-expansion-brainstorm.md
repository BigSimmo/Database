# On Call hub — the logistics content list

Status: **owner-reviewed list, not an approved design.** Nothing here is scheduled.
Date: 2026-09-08 (revised 2026-09-09 — clinical material removed at owner request)
Prior art: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-04-on-call-mode-design.md` (the shipped design)

**Scope: logistics only.** Clinical and Mental Health Act material is already held as documents
in the corpus and is deliberately not duplicated here. Everything below is operational — how the
job works, not how to treat anyone.

---

## The constraint that governs the list

**On Call content is readable by anyone on the internet.** The 2026-09-04 amendment made reads
public; `psychiatry.tools` has no login wall. Only an entry ticked **Personal** stays private.

Two items on the list need a third visibility state — signed-in readers only — before they can
be built at all:

- the teaching meeting link, because a public join link lets strangers into a teaching session
- a reader reporting a wrong number, because that needs an account without edit rights

**A third class needs that same tier before its content is entered, even though the entry itself
already exists.** Physical-access detail is security-sensitive on a world-readable page, and an
earlier draft of this document wrongly cleared everything outside the two items above as safe to
publish. It is not. Mark these Personal, or hold them for the signed-in tier:

- how to get in after hours, and which doors lock when
- on-call room access
- which wards are locked
- anything naming an individual rather than a role

**Never entered in a public field, at any tier:** network or Wi-Fi passwords, door keycodes,
remote-access credentials, or any other secret. Access entries carry the process and the place —
who to ask, where to go, how long it takes — never the credential itself.

A daily roster naming who is on tonight is also not publishable, and is not on this list: link out
to the live rostering system instead.

Handover and orientation entries are safe as **process** — where, when, in what format. They must
never grow into patient lists or identifiable clinical handover, which this mode rules out
entirely.

## Contacts

- Switchboard, per site
- How to page someone, and how to find who is on for another specialty
- The after-hours nurse manager or hospital coordinator — the highest-value number here
- Emergency code numbers, per site, and what to dial for each
- Ward direct numbers, and which wards are locked
- Security: routine and urgent
- IT service desk, and its hours
- Interpreter booking after hours
- Pathology, imaging and blood bank after hours
- After-hours pharmacy
- Bed management and patient flow after hours
- Patient transport booking
- Rostering coordinator, and their working hours
- Payroll
- Occupational health
- Term supervisor and Director of Clinical Training
- A link to the live rostering system, in place of a copied roster

## Logistics

- On-call room: where it is, how to get in, where the linen is
- Food after hours: where, what hours, how to pay
- Parking at night, and how to request a security escort to the car
- Which doors lock at what time, and how to get in after hours
- Mobile phone dead zones, and the nearest landline
- Staff Wi-Fi — how to obtain access, never the password in this field
- Travel between sites: how, and who pays
- Accommodation for regional terms
- Lockers and scrub access
- What you personally may authorise — a taxi, a meal, a security escort

## Forms

Link to the official current version; never keep a copy. A stale form that looks official is worse
than none. For paper-only forms, say where the physical pad or folder lives. Each entry carries
three things: **who signs it, how long it takes, and whether it is online or paper.**

- Timesheet; unrostered overtime; on-call and callback claims
- Travel, mileage and relocation claims
- Professional development allowance claim
- Salary packaging
- Annual leave, sick leave, study leave, parental leave
- Shift swap request
- Keycard, network account, clinical system access, remote access, parking permit
- Term assessment forms and supervision log
- Incident report
- Workers compensation, injury and exposure forms
- Bullying or harassment report
- Downtime paper forms, and where the physical folder is

## Orientation — starting

- Day one: where to go, what time, who to ask for, what to bring
- Pre-start clearances as one checklist: registration, indemnity, immunisation, police check,
  mandatory training
- Access to apply for before starting: keycard, network login, each clinical system, remote
  access, pager or work phone
- The sites you cover, with addresses, and a site map
- What the job actually is: hours, geography, the calls you should expect
- Who to shadow, and who to ask on each ward
- Handover: where, when, in what format, and how you collect the phone or pager
- A "what I wish I'd known" note per rotation, from whoever was there last

## Orientation — finishing

- Rotation dates for the year, and the changeover day
- End-of-term checklist: pager, keycard, locker, badge, outstanding notes, and who signs you off
- Writing a handover for the doctor who replaces you
- Replacing a lost keycard, pager or phone, and whether it costs you

## Who's who

- What each role does and when to call them — nurse practitioner, clinical nurse consultant,
  ANUM, social worker, occupational therapist, peer support worker, consumer advocate
- The on-call ladder in plain administrative terms: who is above you tonight
- WA Health acronyms spelled out

## Teaching

- Weekly teaching, journal club and grand rounds: time and place
- The online meeting link — blocked on the signed-in tier
- Adding the whole series to a calendar in one go
- Who to tell when you cannot attend, and whether attendance is recorded
- Where recordings live, and how to get access
- Assessment timing, and how supervision hours are logged
- Exam dates and application deadlines

## Support

- Employee assistance programme
- Doctors' health service
- Union or AMA
- Taxi home after night shifts: the policy, and who authorises it
- How to request a debrief after a serious incident
- Subpoenas, freedom-of-information and coroner requests: who they go to

## Housekeeping

- Who maintains this hub, and how to reach them
- How a reader reports a wrong number without having edit rights — blocked on the signed-in tier

## Cut as low yield

Recorded so they are decisions rather than oversights: nearby shops and ATMs, bike storage and
EV charging, tea room and fridge rules, stationery ordering, printer codes, room booking, dress
code, supervising students, fire assembly points, media enquiries, superannuation and tax
declaration forms. Each is either handled elsewhere, findable in seconds, or too rare to earn a
place a junior has to scroll past at 3am.

## What needs no code

Everything above except the teaching link and the wrong-number report can be typed into the
existing Contacts, Logistics, Orientation and Education sections today. Forms and Who's who fit
Logistics as two categories; they earn their own sections only if the content grows.
